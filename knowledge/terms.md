# Terms

The canonical glossary of the teaching line *Knowledge, Context and Agentic Engineering for Knowledge Work*. It fixes the wording of the terms that the material uses as load-bearing concepts, so that lecture notes, slide texts and workshop profiles are written and revised against one vocabulary. Entries are ordered by the five modules of the material, preceded by the terms that hold across all of them. Each entry gives the English canonical term, the German equivalent, a definition grounded in the material rather than in general usage, the place in the material where the term does its work, and a literature anchor where a citable source exists.

Source labels are abbreviated. CLARIAH means the CLARIAH-AT lecture notes (`workshops/2026-09-25-clariah-at/lecture-notes.md`), Notes EN the English Full Lecture Notes (`script/full-lecture-notes-en.md`), Skriptum DE the German Full Lecture Notes (`script/full-lecture-notes-de.md`), Deck the Full Slide Deck (`slides/full-slide-deck.md`), VetMedAI the workshop script of the delivered Vetmeduni instance of 2026-04-22. That instance is a past one, so its folder no longer lives in the working tree; the file is preserved in git history at `8d50211` as `workshops/2026-04-22-vetmedai-workshop-1/workshop-script.txt`, and the entries grounded in it stay valid. A German rendering counts as attested where a German source carries it and is marked as a proposal where none does. Literature is cited in short form; the full references live in `drafts/term-references.md` and `drafts/terms-additions.md`, which also hold the working material this glossary condenses.

Where the material contradicts itself, the glossary states one wording as canonical and records the variant. Six such cuts are marked in place as *Editorial decision 2026-08-20*, and each of them is reversible by the operator.

1. Program Query is the canonical term; Program Key is recorded as a superseded coinage of the earlier matrix.
2. Technical Verification means formal conformity and Validation means domain adequacy; the CLARIAH wording is documented as an instance variant with a mapping note.
3. The knowledge document is defined by the wording of the Full Lecture Notes; the CLARIAH wording is recorded as instance wording, and knowledge document, instruction file and agent skill remain three separate entries.
4. Distillation carries an explicit disambiguation against the established machine-learning sense.
5. Autonomy is defined as the Skriptum defines it, with a note on the classical agent-systems property.
6. The anchor sets gain the AI-harness paper (Zhong and Zhu 2026) and the Promptotyping monograph.

## General and Cross-Cutting

Terms that hold across the five modules. The research-data cluster is placed here provisionally; the CLARIAH profile leaves the module assignment of its opening sections undecided, and the cluster would sit equally well at the head of module 1.

- **Knowledge Work.** German Wissensarbeit (proposal). Work paid for the application of theoretical knowledge and the exercise of judgment rather than for manual execution or supervision, with the worker's own definition of the task and continuous learning as its determining conditions. *Source.* Title of the teaching line and Deck title; the material generalises its audience to computer-based, data-driven knowledge work. *Literature.* P. F. Drucker 1959; P. F. Drucker 1999 (DOI 10.2307/41165987); Davenport 2005.
- **Applied Generative AI.** German Angewandte Generative KI (attested through the working-group name in the CLARIAH footnote). The application and adaptation of generative AI methods and systems to problems of a specific research domain, in the tradition of applied computer science, focused on integrating generative models into existing research practice. It names the disciplinary frame inside which the four engineering layers sit. *Source.* CLARIAH, "Applied Generative AI for Research Data Workflows"; Notes EN, same section. *Literature.* No external canonical source; the scripts anchor the term to the author's own framing and to the DHd working group Angewandte Generative KI in den Digitalen Geisteswissenschaften.
- **Research Data.** German Forschungsdaten (Skriptum DE §1; Deck). Representations of research objects, sources, observations or processes that are selected, collected, constructed or produced through scholarly work, shaped by research decisions and dependent on documentation and context for critical assessment, reproducibility and reuse. *Source.* CLARIAH, "Humanities Research Data Are Constructed through Scholarly Workflows"; Notes EN, same section. *Literature.* Geiger 2024 (DOI 10.17175/wp_2023_003_v2); Schöch 2013.
- **Capta.** German Capta, untranslated (absent from the German sources). The argument that humanities data are constructed through situated acts of observation and representation rather than given, so that a transcription already carries decisions about legibility and textual structure. *Source.* CLARIAH, "Humanities Research Data Are Constructed through Scholarly Workflows". *Literature.* J. Drucker 2011 (*Digital Humanities Quarterly* 5.1).
- **Research Data Workflow.** German Forschungsdaten-Workflow (proposal); the Skriptum describes the matter as durchgehender Arbeitszusammenhang (§1.4). The organised process through which research materials and data are created, transformed, enriched, assessed and prepared for scholarly use, including source context, scholarly rules, provenance and the procedures for assessment and acceptance. Automated pipelines perform individual transformations inside it. *Source.* CLARIAH, "Humanities Research Data Are Constructed through Scholarly Workflows" and "AI-Supported Research Data Workflows & Epistemic Infrastructures". *Literature.* Definition of the teaching line, positioned against a pipeline reading.
- **AI Readiness.** German KI-Bereitschaft von Forschungsdaten (proposal). Whether research data are sufficiently structured, documented and governed for effective use in AI-supported workflows, including machine-readable representations, clear provenance and usage conditions, and the information needed to assess quality, uncertainty and potential bias. *Source.* CLARIAH, "Humanities Research Data Are Constructed through Scholarly Workflows"; Notes EN, same section. *Literature.* Majithia et al. 2026 (DOI 10.1002/aaai.70054); Wilkinson et al. 2016 (DOI 10.1038/sdata.2016.18); MLCommons Croissant; GIDA, CARE Principles.
- **Provenance.** German Provenienz (Skriptum DE §1.4, §4.4, §5.4, §5.8, §7.2). The documented relation between a source, the operations applied to it and the resulting representation, required wherever generated artefacts are stored separately from unchanged originals. It is separate from the question of validity. *Source.* Skriptum DE §7.2 and §5.4 as a property of a knowledge document; CLARIAH, "Prepare the Working Environment before the First Prompt". *Literature.* No anchor in the mined texts; the module map records PROV-O and the Web Annotation Data Model as the formal layer of the Skriptum's chapter 1.

## 1 Understanding Large Language Models

### Capability landscape

- **Frontier Model.** German Frontier-LLM (Deck). A currently leading general-purpose model whose combination of multimodality, reasoning, coding, tool use and agentic capability the scripts treat as qualitatively relevant for computational research, without claiming that it is the appropriate choice for every task. *Source.* CLARIAH, "Applied Generative AI for Research Data Workflows" and "The Current LLM Capability Landscape"; Notes EN, abstract and "Which Frontier Models and AI Technologies Do You Use?". *Literature.* Anderljung et al. 2023 (arXiv:2307.03718), proposed anchor for the received definition; the scripts use the Artificial Analysis leaderboard as the moving reference point.
- **Capability Frontier.** German Leistungsgrenze aktueller Modelle (proposal). The current outer edge of measurable model capability across reasoning, coding, agentic tasks, speed and cost, located through continuously updated comparisons rather than through a single benchmark score. *Source.* CLARIAH, "The Current LLM Capability Landscape" and "Frontier LLMs Asymmetrically Amplify Computational Research". *Literature.* Artificial Analysis LLM Leaderboard; METR, Task-Completion Time Horizons.
- **Asymmetric Amplification.** German Asymmetrische Amplifikation (Deck). The thesis that frontier models strengthen existing human capability unevenly, most strongly where relevant knowledge is externalised, research objects are digitally represented, actions can be executed through software and the environment returns meaningful feedback. It is the overarching thesis organising the capability block of this module. *Source.* CLARIAH and Notes EN, "Frontier LLMs Asymmetrically Amplify Computational Research"; Deck, thesis slide. *Literature.* Pollin 2026, "Asymmetric Amplification" (Digital Humanities Craft).
- **Task-Completion Time Horizon.** German Aufgabenhorizont (proposal). The duration of software tasks that an agent can complete at a specified success probability, used as one capability signal among several and explicitly qualified as no general measure of research capability. *Source.* CLARIAH, "Frontier LLMs Asymmetrically Amplify Computational Research" with footnote 26; Notes EN, same section. *Literature.* Kinniment et al. 2025 (arXiv:2503.14499); METR, Task-Completion Time Horizons.
- **Jagged Capability (Jagged Alien Intelligence).** German Jagged Alien Intelligence, untranslated in the Deck slide title; no rendering in the Skriptum. The observed capability profile in which a model performs extremely difficult tasks while failing on neighbouring tasks that appear simpler to a human observer, which blocks both naive extrapolation from single successes and dismissal from conspicuous failures. *Source.* CLARIAH and Notes EN, "LLMs as Jagged Alien 'Intelligences'"; Deck. *Literature.* Dell'Acqua et al. 2026 (DOI 10.1287/orsc.2025.21838); Gans 2026 (arXiv:2601.07573); Summerfield 2025.
- **Confabulation.** German Konfabulation (Deck). The production of plausible but unsupported claims, chosen in the scripts as the descriptive term for this behaviour in place of hallucination. The glossary states the substitution, since the adjacent received literature runs under hallucination. *Source.* CLARIAH, "LLMs as Jagged Alien 'Intelligences'", which states the terminological choice explicitly; Deck. *Literature.* No canonical source for the term in this sense; Ji et al. 2023 (DOI 10.1145/3571730) is the standard survey under the received name.
- **Sycophancy.** German Sycophancy, untranslated, with the Deck gloss Tendenz von LLMs, Nutzer:innen zuzustimmen. The tendency to align an answer with a belief the user has expressed even where this reduces factual accuracy, with the methodological consequence that a prompt asking for confirmation of a preferred interpretation is weak evidence. *Source.* CLARIAH and Notes EN, "LLMs as Jagged Alien 'Intelligences'" and the countermeasure in the prompting principles; Deck. *Literature.* Sharma et al. 2023 (arXiv:2310.13548).

### How language models work

- **Large Language Model.** German Large Language Model, used untranslated, with the Skriptum gloss probabilistisches Textsystem. A neural network trained on large tokenised collections to estimate a probability distribution over the next token given the tokens in its context, whose output stays probabilistic across repeated runs of the same input. *Source.* Notes EN, "How Large Language Models Work"; Skriptum DE §2.1. *Literature.* Brown et al. 2020 (arXiv:2005.14165); Vaswani et al. 2017 (arXiv:1706.03762).
- **Next Token Prediction.** German Next Token Prediction, untranslated (Skriptum DE §2.1). The training objective of autoregressive language modelling, which the scripts separate sharply from the capabilities acquired while optimising it, since the objective asks which continuation is probable rather than whether a proposition is true. *Source.* Notes EN, "How Large Language Models Work"; Skriptum DE §2.1 with footnote 1 marking the description as a functional simplification. *Literature.* Brown et al. 2020 (arXiv:2005.14165).
- **Transformer Architecture and Self-Attention.** German Transformer-Modell (Skriptum DE, footnote 1); Transformer-Architektur is a proposal. The layered architecture in which attention lets information at different positions of the sequence influence one another, so that a token receives a context-dependent representation rather than an isolated one. It carries the bridge to module 3, that a prompt intervenes in the current computation because it changes the sequence the attention operates over. *Source.* Notes EN, "Transformer Architecture"; Deck, context-window slide. *Literature.* Vaswani et al. 2017 (arXiv:1706.03762).
- **Token and Tokenization.** German Token, Tokenisierung (Skriptum DE §4.3). The discrete units into which a tokenizer converts character sequences before they enter the network, with the consequence that context limits, input cost and output length are measured in tokens rather than in words. *Source.* Notes EN, "Tokenization"; Skriptum DE §4.3 in the path from file to context window. *Literature.* Sennrich, Haddow and Birch 2016 (arXiv:1508.07909).
- **Embedding and Contextual Representation.** German Embedding und kontextuelle Repräsentation (proposal); the Skriptum paraphrases as hochdimensionale numerische Repräsentationen (§3.6). The mapping of discrete token identifiers into a high-dimensional vector space, and the repeated transformation of those initial vectors across the network into representations that depend on the surrounding tokens and the current task. It supplies the mechanism behind the claim that wording matters without assuming one fixed meaning per token. *Source.* Notes EN, "Embeddings and Contextual Representations"; Skriptum DE §3.6. *Literature.* Vaswani et al. 2017 (arXiv:1706.03762).
- **Pretraining.** German Pre-Training (Skriptum DE §2.2). The training stage on large heterogeneous corpora that establishes broad linguistic, conceptual and procedural representations, described as building a repertoire while noting that knowledge and capability stay entangled. *Source.* Notes EN, "Pretraining and Posttraining" and "Training Builds and Shapes the Latent Program Space"; Skriptum DE §2.2; CLARIAH, "Training Shapes the Latent Program Space". *Literature.* Brown et al. 2020 (arXiv:2005.14165).
- **Posttraining.** German Post-Training (Skriptum DE §2.2). The training stages that shape how the learned repertoire is expressed in an assistant, through instruction tuning, demonstrations, preference learning, reinforcement learning and related methods, including interface capabilities such as tool calling. *Source.* Notes EN, "Pretraining and Posttraining"; Skriptum DE §2.2. *Literature.* Ouyang et al. 2022 (arXiv:2203.02155).
- **Midtraining (Model Spec Midtraining).** German Midtraining (proposal), absent from both German sources. A stage between broad pretraining and later alignment training in which a model is trained on synthetic documents discussing a Model Spec or Constitution, in order to influence how the subsequent alignment training generalises. The scripts mark it as a proposed technique rather than a standardised phase. *Source.* Notes EN, "Training Builds and Shapes the Latent Program Space". *Literature.* Li et al. 2026, "Model Spec Midtraining" (arXiv:2605.02087).
- **Parametric Knowledge.** German parametrisches Wissen (proposal); the Skriptum describes the matter in §2.2 without naming it. Knowledge a model can produce because its parameters encode statistical structure from training material, which the scripts contrast with an addressable copy of a training document and therefore with any direct provenance for a generated claim. It grounds the provenance argument of module 5. *Source.* Notes EN, "The Shape of a Wikipedia Article About Zebras" with its three information layers of model parameters, external resources and current context; Skriptum DE §2.2. *Literature.* Petroni et al. 2019 (arXiv:1909.01066), proposed anchor; the scripts use a lossy-compression analogy and mark it explicitly as an analogy.
- **In-Context Learning.** German In-Context Learning (proposal), absent from both German sources. The ability of a model to adapt its behaviour to instructions, examples and information supplied in the current context without any change to model parameters. It is what makes context a working surface at all. *Source.* Notes EN, "Context Engineering Shapes the Model's Working Context" and the training and inference distinction in "Pretraining and Posttraining". *Literature.* Brown et al. 2020 (arXiv:2005.14165); Xie et al. 2022 (arXiv:2111.02080).

### Latent program space

- **Latent Program Space.** German Latent Program Space, untranslated (Skriptum DE §3.6, marked there as a metaphor). The space of possible learned transformations a model can activate depending on input and context, read in the material as a conceptual model of learned processing routines rather than as a delimited store of classical programs. *Source.* Notes EN, "Prompt Engineering: Finding Coordinates in a Latent Program Space" with the five-item terminology list; CLARIAH, "Prompt Engineering as Search in a Latent Program Space"; Skriptum DE §3.6 with figure 7. *Literature.* Chollet 2019 (arXiv:1911.01547); Bonnet and Macfarlane 2024 (arXiv:2411.08706); Chollet 2023, "How I Think About LLM Prompt Engineering".
- **Vector Program.** German Vektorprogramm (proposal); the Skriptum paraphrases as gelernte Verarbeitungsroutinen (§3.6). A learned and distributed transformation across high-dimensional representations that realises one capability or behaviour, distinguished explicitly from a discrete symbolic program stored inside the model. *Source.* Notes EN and CLARIAH, five-item terminology list; Skriptum DE §3.6. *Literature.* Chollet 2023.
- **Program Query.** German Programmabfrage (proposal), absent from both German sources. The prompt considered as an address or search signal into the learned repertoire, where one part of a prompt steers towards a region of that repertoire and another supplies the material the selected transformation operates on. *Source.* Notes EN and CLARIAH, five-item terminology list. *Literature.* Chollet 2023; Xie et al. 2022 (arXiv:2111.02080); Reynolds and McDonell 2021 (DOI 10.1145/3411763.3451760). *Editorial decision 2026-08-20.* Program Query is canonical. Both canonical scripts carry it verbatim in their terminology lists, while Program Key appears in no text of the teaching line and was a coinage of the earlier terminology matrix; it is recorded here as a superseded variant so that older notes remain resolvable.
- **Reasoning and Test-Time Compute.** German Reasoning, on the Deck as Thinking Token; the Skriptum names Reasoning in passing (§3.5). The allocation of additional computation during inference before a final answer is committed, read as a partial shift of the search burden from external prompt variation into a single model invocation. It is the fifth item of the latent-program-space terminology list. *Source.* CLARIAH, "Reasoning and Test-Time Compute as Internal Search"; Notes EN, terminology list and the principle "Use Reasoning Selectively". *Literature.* Snell et al. 2024 (arXiv:2408.03314).
- **Mechanistic Interpretability.** German Mechanistische Interpretierbarkeit (Skriptum DE §3.6, as Interpretierbarkeitsverfahren). Research into the internal computations through which a model produces behaviour, used in the material for the limited claim that model computation is structured and input-dependent. It supplies no empirical map of a latent program space. *Source.* CLARIAH, "Excursus: Mechanistic Interpretability"; Notes EN, "Excursus: Mechanistic Interpretability and Activation Paths"; Skriptum DE §3.6 with figures 7 and 8. *Literature.* Lindsey et al. 2025 (Transformer Circuits); Sofroniew et al. 2026 (Transformer Circuits).

### Assistant layer

- **Assistant Character.** German Assistentenfigur (Skriptum DE §2.2). The conversational persona a provider shapes and stabilises through training, runtime instructions, policy layers and product design, kept separate from the underlying model and from any claim about subjective experience. *Source.* CLARIAH and Notes EN, "The Assistant Is a Character Generated by the Model"; Skriptum DE §2.2 with figure 3. *Literature.* Anthropic, "Claude's Character"; Anthropic 2026, "Claude's Constitution".
- **Constitution, Character Training and System Prompt.** German Systeminstruktionen (Skriptum DE §2.2); Constitution untranslated in footnote 2. Three layers the scripts insist on separating, a training artefact such as a Constitution or Model Spec that shapes what is learned, character training and post-training that stabilise behavioural dispositions, and a system prompt that operates at runtime inside one deployment. *Source.* Notes EN, "How Anthropic Shapes Claude's Assistant Character"; Skriptum DE §2.2 with footnote 2 marking the ontological reading as contested. *Literature.* Anthropic 2026, "Claude's New Constitution"; Anthropic, "System Prompts".

### Multimodality

- **Vision-Language Model.** German Vision-Language-Modell (proposal); the Skriptum speaks of multimodaler Verarbeitung (§4.3). A model that processes visual and linguistic information within the same task, so that a general-purpose multimodal system can transcribe a facsimile from image and instruction without being a dedicated OCR or HTR system. *Source.* CLARIAH, "Multimodality and Vision-Language Models"; Notes EN, "Multimodality & Vision Language Models". *Literature.* Gemini Team 2023 (arXiv:2312.11805).
- **Contextually Plausible Reading.** German kontextuell plausible Lesung (proposal); the Skriptum describes the failure in §2.1 without naming it. The characteristic failure mode of transcription by a general multimodal model, where the generated reading fits the linguistic context while the visual evidence of the source supports a different one, so that fluency and internal coherence carry no information about source fidelity. It is a coinage of the teaching line. *Source.* CLARIAH, "Example Result and Critical Review" and "Multimodality and Vision-Language Models"; Notes EN, "Hands on 1 (Example Result)". *Literature.* No canonical source.
- **Emergent Capability.** German Emergenz (Deck, in the open question on zero-shot transcription). A capability that appears without having been trained for separately, used in the scripts as an open question about zero-shot handwriting recognition rather than as an established classification, because model scale and training composition are not observable for proprietary systems. *Source.* CLARIAH, "Example Result and Critical Review", closing question; Notes EN, "Multimodality & Vision Language Models". *Literature.* Wei et al. 2022 (arXiv:2206.07682) and the counterposition in Schaeffer, Miranda and Koyejo 2023 (arXiv:2304.15004), both proposed anchors that the scripts do not cite.

## 2 Prompt Engineering

- **Prompt Engineering.** German Prompt Engineering, untranslated (Skriptum DE, chapter 3). The iterative development of individual prompts by changing their content, their structure or the prompting techniques applied to them. The material defines the term deliberately more narrowly than context engineering, since it addresses one input sequence rather than the whole information environment of a longer working trajectory, and it replaces neither missing domain knowledge nor missing project context. *Source.* Skriptum DE §3.1 with figure 4; Notes EN, "Prompting Strategies"; CLARIAH, "Prompt Engineering". *Literature.* Schulhoff et al. 2024 (arXiv:2406.06608); Liu et al. 2023 (DOI 10.1145/3560815); Reynolds and McDonell 2021 (DOI 10.1145/3411763.3451760); Anthropic, Prompt engineering overview.
- **Prompt.** German Prompt, with the Skriptum gloss die für einen Modellaufruf bereitgestellte Eingabesequenz (§3.1). The input sequence supplied for one model call, which may contain a task, source material, context, requirements, constraints, examples, procedural instructions and specifications for the expected output. *Source.* Skriptum DE §3.1; CLARIAH, "Prompt Engineering". *Literature.* Schulhoff et al. 2024 (arXiv:2406.06608).
- **Bounded Specification.** German Begrenzte Spezifikation (Skriptum DE §3.2). A prompt understood as a specification of a current task inside an already existing knowledge and working context, which points at persistent project rules instead of restating them and therefore stays compact while remaining precise. It is the hinge between module 2 and module 3, since the prompt can be bounded only where a maintained knowledge base exists to point at. *Source.* Skriptum DE §3.2 with the TEI example and §3.8; Notes EN, "Transcribe a Facsimile with Gemini 3.7 Flash". *Literature.* In-house formulation of the teaching line.
- **Task, Context, Rules, Output.** German list of seven parts in the Skriptum, Ziel, Ausgangslage, Anforderungen, Einschränkungen, Vorgehen, Ausgabeform, Abschlusskriterium (§3.2). The four-part working structure for research prompts, separating what the model should do, which source or project information it needs, which scholarly and technical constraints govern the result, and in which form the result is returned, so that the parts can be revised independently. The English texts teach four parts and the Skriptum seven; both lists are instance-dependent renderings of the same separation, and the glossary states neither as exclusive. *Source.* CLARIAH, "Prompt Engineering" and the two transcription prompts; Notes EN, "Prompting Strategies", item Structure the Prompt; Skriptum DE §3.2. *Literature.* Schulhoff et al. 2024 (arXiv:2406.06608); Google, Prompt Design Strategies.
- **Role Prompting.** German Role Prompting (Skriptum DE §3.3). A brief functional assignment of a role to the model, which can influence terminology, style, perspective and level of detail while adding no factual knowledge, so that it does not reliably improve accuracy or reasoning. *Source.* Skriptum DE §3.3; CLARIAH, "Prompting Is Weird"; Notes EN, "Prompting Is Weird, and Keeps Changing". *Literature.* Basil et al. 2025 (arXiv:2512.05858); Hu and Collier 2024 (arXiv:2402.10811).
- **Persona Prompting (Persona Engineering).** German Persona Prompting (Skriptum DE §3.3); Persona Engineering on the Deck slide. A worked-out perspective with background, experience, goals, constraints and usage situation, used as a structured viewpoint for reviewing an artefact. Its outputs are hypotheses about users and carry no empirical user data. The Skriptum keeps role and persona prompting distinct and warns against equating them. *Source.* Skriptum DE §3.3 with the workshop-participant persona; Notes EN, "Persona Engineering"; Deck. *Literature.* Hu and Collier 2024 (arXiv:2402.10811).
- **Self-Revision.** German Self-Revision (Skriptum DE §3.4). A staged interaction in which the model first produces a draft, then checks it against explicit criteria, then revises only the confirmed findings. This surfaces errors while constituting no independent verification, since the same model can miss or rationalise its own assumptions. *Source.* Skriptum DE §3.4; Deck, hands-on step 3; Notes EN, "Generate, Compare and Reconcile". *Literature.* Zheng et al. 2023 (arXiv:2306.05685), proposed anchor for the self-enhancement limit.
- **Structured Output.** German Strukturierte Ausgaben (Skriptum DE §3.4). An output constrained to a defined format such as JSON, XML, a Markdown table or a file structure, which reduces ambiguity and enables deterministic checks. The material separates four levels of correctness, syntactic conformity, structural completeness, semantic correctness and scholarly adequacy, and states that the first two prove nothing about the last. *Source.* Skriptum DE §3.4; CLARIAH, "From Generated Text to Structured Data"; Notes EN, ".txt → JSON → CSV". *Literature.* The four levels are a distinction of the material.
- **Prompt Sensitivity.** German Promptwirkungen, with the qualifier modell-, aufgaben- und sprachabhängig (Skriptum DE §3.5). The empirical finding that model output can change in response to linguistic details with little relation to the task, so that prompting effects are local to model, version, task, language, position, metric and random variation and are treated as experimental interventions rather than as transferable rules. *Source.* Skriptum DE §3.5 with figures 5 and 6 and the seven-step evaluation procedure; CLARIAH and Notes EN, "Prompting Is Weird". *Literature.* Li et al. 2023 (arXiv:2307.11760); Yin et al. 2024 (arXiv:2402.14531); Battle and Gollapudi 2024 (arXiv:2402.10949); Rajeev et al. 2025 (arXiv:2503.01781).

## 3 Knowledge and Context Engineering

### Context engineering

- **Context Engineering.** German Context Engineering, untranslated (Skriptum DE, chapter 4). The systematic selection, organisation, maintenance and provision of the task-specific information state for a model call or an agentic run. It determines which information a model receives, in which form and order it arrives, when further information is loaded, and what deliberately stays outside the current context. *Source.* Skriptum DE §4.1; CLARIAH, "Context Engineering Shapes the Working Context"; Notes EN, "Context Engineering Shapes the Model's Working Context". *Literature.* Mei et al. 2025, cited by Skriptum DE §4.1; Karpathy 2025, coinage post; Anthropic 2025, "Effective context engineering for AI agents"; LangChain 2025, four-part strategy taxonomy.
- **Context Window.** German Context Window (Skriptum DE §4.2); Kontextfenster (Deck). The finite token-based processing space of one model call, containing the supplied input and those generated tokens that remain in the active sequence. Its nominal size is a technical limit and no target for how much information to supply. *Source.* Skriptum DE §4.2; CLARIAH, "Context Engineering Shapes the Working Context"; Notes EN, "Context Window, Context Rot and Distillation"; Deck. *Literature.* Vaswani et al. 2017 (arXiv:1706.03762).
- **Working Context.** German Working Context, untranslated (Skriptum DE §4.5 and §4.6). The task-specific configuration of information, documents, data access, instructions, tool descriptions, permissions, current state and feedback assembled for one assignment. Not every component is serialised into tokens, since an agent may reach a dataset through a tool while only selected results enter the model context. *Source.* Skriptum DE §4.5 with the worked example for one edition page and §4.6 with the four-way loading matrix; Notes EN, "Project Knowledge Base and Working Context"; Deck. *Literature.* Pollin, *Promptotyping* (monograph, review draft 0.9, 2026).
- **Context Rot.** German Context Rot, untranslated (Skriptum DE §4.2; Deck). Observed degradation in a model's ability to retrieve and apply relevant information as the active context becomes longer, denser or more distracting. The scripts refuse to reduce it to one causal mechanism and name position effects, distractors, conflicting information and obsolete intermediate state as contributors. *Source.* Skriptum DE §4.2; CLARIAH, "Context Engineering Shapes the Working Context"; Notes EN, "Context Rot"; Deck, with the caveat that the reported evidence is task-specific. *Literature.* Hong, Troynikov and Huber 2025, *Context Rot* (Chroma).
- **Dense and Sufficient Context.** German Dichter und hinreichender Kontext (Skriptum DE §4.2; Deck). The stated target quantity for a working context, as bounded as possible while carrying every distinction the task requires. The scripts argue against a shortening rule, because radical reduction removes provenance, conditions and uncertainty. *Source.* Skriptum DE §4.2, closing paragraph; Notes EN, "Why AI Agents Need Context"; Deck. *Literature.* Formulation of the teaching line derived from the Context Rot finding.
- **Context Compression.** German Context Compression, untranslated (Skriptum DE §4.4; Deck). Reduction of the amount of information entering an active context, through selection of relevant passages, summary, removal of repetition, aggregation of data, retention of representative examples or compaction of the previous working history. *Source.* Skriptum DE §4.4; Notes EN, "Selecting, Structuring and Distilling Knowledge"; Deck. *Literature.* Pollin, *Promptotyping* (monograph, review draft 0.9, 2026).
- **Distillation.** German Distillation, untranslated (Skriptum DE §4.4 and §7.4; Deck). The stronger operation beyond compression, transforming an existing understanding into a selective, structured and inspectable representation that preserves the concepts, relations, conditions, uncertainties and rationales a given purpose requires. The same material is distilled differently for a general introduction, an implementation task and a verification task. *Source.* Skriptum DE §4.4 with its three operations of selection, structuring and condensation, and §7.4 as a Promptotyping phase; Notes EN, "Selecting, Structuring and Distilling Knowledge"; Deck. *Literature.* Pollin, *Promptotyping* (monograph, review draft 0.9, 2026). *Editorial decision 2026-08-20.* The sense used here is canonical inside this teaching line and is disambiguated explicitly against the established machine-learning sense of knowledge distillation, where a smaller model is trained on the outputs of a larger one. The sense used here operates on documents and understanding, the machine-learning sense on model parameters, and the audience meets both.
- **Over-Distillation.** German Überdestillation (Skriptum DE §4.4). The counter-risk of distillation, present when provenance, uncertainty or details needed for action are lost, so that a shorter document turns several alternative statements into one apparently unambiguous rule. *Source.* Skriptum DE §4.4. *Literature.* Term of the teaching line.

### Knowledge engineering

- **Knowledge Engineering.** German Knowledge Engineering, untranslated (Skriptum DE, chapter 5). The construction and maintenance of explicit, inspectable and revisable project knowledge, which makes the relevant concepts, rules, decisions, constraints and uncertainties visible enough to be read, criticised and continued. Its aim is purpose-bound rather than a complete representation of all available information. *Source.* Skriptum DE §5.1; Notes EN, "Why Knowledge Must Be Made Explicit"; CLARIAH, knowledge-engineering section. *Literature.* Studer, Benjamins and Fensel 1998 (DOI 10.1016/S0169-023X(97)00056-6); Newell 1982 (DOI 10.1016/0004-3702(82)90012-1); Schreiber et al. 2000, CommonKADS.
- **Knowledge Acquisition.** German Knowledge Acquisition, untranslated (Skriptum DE §5.2); Wissenserwerb on the Deck. The elicitation and explicitation of relevant knowledge from two sources, existing documents and data on one side, implicit knowledge of people and organisations on the other, through document analysis, interviews, workshops, observation of workflows, error analysis and joint modelling sessions. *Source.* Skriptum DE §5.2 with the `<supplied>` example of an unwritten but practised rule; Notes EN, "Scholar Centred Design and Requirements Engineering"; Deck. *Literature.* Studer, Benjamins and Fensel 1998; Schreiber et al. 2000, CommonKADS.
- **Tacit Knowledge and Externalisation.** German Implizites Wissen (Skriptum DE §5.1 and §5.2); the Deck uses the slide title "I know things". Knowledge held by people, teams or institutions without being documented in a form another person or system can inspect. The material locates the starting point of knowledge engineering in the gap between knowledge possessed and knowledge operationally available. *Source.* Notes EN, "'I Know Things'" and "Why Knowledge Must Be Made Explicit"; Skriptum DE §5.1; Deck. *Literature.* Nonaka 1994 (DOI 10.1287/orsc.5.1.14).
- **Knowledge Modelling.** German Wissensmodellierung (Deck). The classical construction of a knowledge base by identifying the concepts of a domain, representing them formally and making them queryable, named as one of three neighbouring traditions that AI-supported knowledge work draws on. The material states the shift from the classical form explicitly, that the formalisation target is structured natural language with light metadata rather than logic and ontology, because the language model supplies the language understanding. *Source.* Notes EN, "Knowledge Modelling, Personal Information Management and Project Management"; Deck, three-tradition slide. *Literature.* Russell and Norvig 2020; Allen et al. 2023 (DOI 10.4230/TGDK.1.1.3).
- **Personal Information Management.** German Personal Information Management, untranslated (Deck). The study of how people acquire, organise, maintain, retrieve, use and share information across formats and locations, with fragmentation as its core problem and the personal information collection as its core concept. *Source.* Notes EN, "Knowledge Modelling, Personal Information Management and Project Management"; Deck. *Literature.* Jones, Dinneen, Capra, Diekema and Pérez-Quiñones 2017 (DOI 10.1081/E-ELIS4-120053695).
- **Project Knowledge Base.** German Project Knowledge Base, untranslated (Skriptum DE §4.5 and §5.3), with the descriptive gloss Wissensbasis. The persistent, inspectable and revisable body of documented project knowledge holding the project's current understanding of its data, its purpose, its requirements, decisions and uncertainties. It describes and contextualises sources and research data and replaces neither. *Source.* Skriptum DE §5.3 with the concrete `knowledge/` folder layout; Notes EN, "Project Knowledge Base and Working Context"; Deck. *Literature.* Pollin, *Promptotyping* (monograph, review draft 0.9, 2026).
- **Knowledge-Base Governance and Curation.** German Governance und Kuration (Skriptum DE §5.7). Governance sets the rules for building, changing and using a knowledge base; curation applies those rules to the actual holdings, structurally for names, metadata, links, document types, versions and duplicates, and substantively for contradictions, outdated rules, missing constraints and over-condensed passages. An agent may locate problems and propose changes while substantive changes require human responsibility. *Source.* Skriptum DE §5.7. *Literature.* Schreiber et al. 2000, CommonKADS, proposed anchor for the maintenance argument.

### Document classes

- **Knowledge Document.** German Wissensdokument (Skriptum DE §5.4). A bounded, structured and revisable representation of relevant knowledge, distilled from a larger body, checked by people and usable as context by LLM-based systems. Its properties are a clearly delimited subject, a traceable structure and dual readability for people and models. *Source.* Skriptum DE §5.4 with the definition and the property list; Notes EN, knowledge-document section; CLARIAH, "Markdown and Knowledge Documents". *Literature.* Pollin 2025, "Promptotyping. Von der Idee zur Anwendung" for the document class; Nonaka 1994 (DOI 10.1287/orsc.5.1.14) for externalisation as a lossy and cyclical conversion. *Editorial decision 2026-08-20.* The wording of the Full Lecture Notes is canonical, bounded, structured and revisable. The CLARIAH wording, an atomic, structured and verifiable unit of externalised project knowledge, is recorded as instance wording of that profile; the VetMedAI script carries the same atomic reading in German. Atomic and verifiable make claims that wording deliberately does not make, since a knowledge document is bounded by purpose rather than by size and is revised rather than proven.
- **Instruction File.** German Instruktionsdatei (Skriptum DE §5.6). A persistent Markdown file such as `AGENTS.md` or `CLAUDE.md` that sets recurring rules for agentic work in a project and is loaded at session start, so that stable working rules are not retyped into every prompt. Discovery and precedence rules differ across tools, and such a file is context rather than an enforcement mechanism. *Source.* Skriptum DE §5.6 with the four-way distinction of knowledge document, instruction file, skill and prompt; Notes EN, "AGENTS.md and CLAUDE.md"; Deck. *Literature.* AGENTS.md; Anthropic, "How Claude Remembers Your Project". *Editorial decision 2026-08-20.* Kept as a separate entry from the knowledge document, following the four-way distinction of the scripts, where a knowledge document describes an object and an instruction file governs recurring work.
- **Agent Skill and Progressive Disclosure.** German Agent Skill, untranslated (Skriptum DE §5.6; Deck). A reusable folder holding a `SKILL.md` and optional scripts, references and assets for one recurring class of task, loaded in stages so that only name and description occupy the context until the skill becomes relevant. It is procedural where a knowledge document is descriptive. *Source.* Skriptum DE §5.6 with edition-specific examples; Notes EN, "Agent Skill"; Deck. *Literature.* Agent Skills specification (agentskills.io); Anthropic, Agent Skills overview. *Editorial decision 2026-08-20.* Progressive disclosure stays with the skill, which is the artefact carrying the mechanism, and is no longer cited as an anchor of the knowledge-document definition.
- **Markdown as Serialization.** German Markdown als technische Repräsentation (Skriptum DE §5.5); the Deck phrases it as Serialisierung. The claim that the knowledge document is the conceptual artefact while Markdown is one possible technical representation of it, chosen for openness, versionability, linkability and direct readability by people and models. The material makes no claim of superiority over richer formats and keeps TEI, JSON, RDF, XML, schemas and databases for cases requiring richer semantics or formal constraints. *Source.* Skriptum DE §5.5; CLARIAH, "Markdown and Knowledge Documents"; Notes EN, "Markdown Makes Document Structure Explicit for LLMs"; Deck. *Literature.* Markdown Guide.

## 4 Agentic Engineering

### Agents, harness and execution

- **Agentic Engineering.** German Agentic Engineering, untranslated (Skriptum DE, chapter 6). The systematic organisation and control of multi-step agentic work, covering the delimitation and decomposition of tasks, tool use, the handling of intermediate results, states and handovers, stopping and escalation conditions, and checking and continuation. The material scope is wider than agentic software development, since the work addresses data descriptions, specifications, mappings, design decisions, process documents and verification concepts alongside code. *Source.* Skriptum DE §6.1; Notes EN, "Agentic Engineering"; Deck, which states the widening explicitly. *Literature.* Karpathy 2026, Sequoia Ascent account of the coinage; AGENT 2026 workshop at ICSE; Hassan et al. 2025 (arXiv:2509.06216); Sapkota, Roumeliotis and Karkee 2025 (DOI 10.1016/j.inffus.2025.103599); Pollin, *Promptotyping* (monograph, review draft 0.9, 2026) for the operative definition.
- **AI Agent and Agentic AI.** German AI Agent, untranslated (Skriptum DE §2.3); Agentic AI untranslated. An agent pursues a goal across several model and tool calls, examining its environment, selecting an action, observing the result and updating its approach, with the model as the flexible planning and interpretation module whose actual scope of action arises from tools and a working environment. The received distinction treats agentic systems as the umbrella, workflows as orchestration through predefined code paths, and agents as systems that direct their own processes and tool use; agentic AI names the wider paradigm of multi-agent collaboration, dynamic task decomposition, persistent memory and coordinated autonomy. *Source.* Skriptum DE §2.3 with the nine-step edition example; Notes EN and Deck; VetMedAI, chatbot against agent contrast. *Literature.* Anthropic 2024, "Building effective agents"; Wooldridge and Jennings 1995 (DOI 10.1017/S0269888900008122); Sapkota, Roumeliotis and Karkee 2025 (DOI 10.1016/j.inffus.2025.103599).
- **AI Harness.** German AI Harness, untranslated (Skriptum DE §2.4). The technical software layer through which an LLM-based agent receives context, calls tools, accesses files, executes programs and processes feedback, and which sets what may be read or changed, which commands run without confirmation, how tool output returns into the model context, how long a run continues, when a person is involved and how intermediate results are stored. It provides technical control and decides nothing about scholarly adequacy. *Source.* Skriptum DE §2.4 with the control list and the validator example; CLARIAH, "Agentic Engineering and AI Harnesses"; Notes EN, "AI Harness Architecture". *Literature.* Zhong and Zhu 2026, "AI Harness Engineering" (arXiv:2605.13357); Anthropic 2025, "Effective harnesses for long-running agents"; Martin 2026, "Agent Harness Design"; METR 2024, capability elicitation. *Editorial decision 2026-08-20.* The anchor sets are merged. Zhong and Zhu is the paper all four mined source texts use for the harness and was missing from the earlier matrix; the practitioner and evaluation anchors are kept for the component-level and measurement-level readings.
- **Compound System (Model, Harness and Environment).** German Verbundsystem (proposal); the Skriptum states the matter in §2.3 and §2.4 without a term. The claim that agentic capability is a property of model, harness and environment together, so that the unit of analysis is the complete system of model, harness, context, tools, state, permissions, executable environment and feedback. *Source.* CLARIAH, "Agentic Engineering and AI Harnesses" with the explicit formula; Notes EN, "AI Harness Architecture"; Deck. *Literature.* Zhong and Zhu 2026 (arXiv:2605.13357).
- **Tool Use.** German Werkzeugnutzung (Skriptum DE §6.1 and §6.4); Tool Use on the Deck. The connection of a model to executable functions and external resources such as file access, terminal, code execution, web search, database queries, browser control, validators and specialised APIs. The material values it most where a tool returns evidence about the consequences of an action. *Source.* Skriptum DE §6.4; Notes EN, "Tool Use"; CLARIAH, on tool use changing the effective system; Deck. *Literature.* Sapkota, Roumeliotis and Karkee 2025 (DOI 10.1016/j.inffus.2025.103599).
- **Trajectory.** German Arbeitstrajektorie (Skriptum DE §3.7 and §6.1; Deck). The complete sequence of observations, intermediate decisions, tool calls, file modifications, execution results and follow-up actions that an agentic run produces, which the material makes the object of engineering in place of the single response. *Source.* Notes EN, "Agentic Engineering" with the shift from response to trajectory; Skriptum DE §6.1 with the propagating-error example; Deck. *Literature.* Zhong and Zhu 2026 (arXiv:2605.13357), for the argument that a final artefact is weak evidence about the process that produced it.
- **Agentic Execution Loop.** German Agentische Ausführungsschleife (Skriptum DE §6.2 with figure 10). The repeated cycle of registering the current state, planning a bounded next step, executing a tool or action, observing the result and updating the approach, bound to documented requirements, permissions, stopping conditions and human intervention points. *Source.* Skriptum DE §6.2 with the ten-step edition example; Notes EN, "Agentic Engineering"; Deck, hands-on step 1. *Literature.* No single canonical source; the Deck relates the loop to the cybernetic feedback loop (Wiener 1948) and notes the difference that feedback arrives through tool output rather than sensors.
- **Autonomy.** German Autonomie (Skriptum DE §6.2; Deck). The extent of work performed between two human interventions. Autonomy in this sense is compatible with full human control. *Source.* Skriptum DE §6.2, closing sentence; Deck, on the classical four properties. *Literature.* Wooldridge and Jennings 1995 (DOI 10.1017/S0269888900008122). *Editorial decision 2026-08-20.* The Skriptum reading is canonical, a scope of work between two human interventions. This is a deliberate narrowing against the classical agent-systems property, where autonomy is a system property alongside reactivity, pro-activeness and social ability. Both readings are defensible and they are not congruent; a text using the classical sense states it.

### Control and delegation

- **Least-Privilege Tool Access.** German Prinzip der geringsten erforderlichen Berechtigung (Skriptum DE §6.4). The rule that tool and file access is granted at the minimum required scope, with source files readable but not writable, generated artefacts confined to a working folder, validators running without confirmation, and publication or deployment steps requiring explicit release. *Source.* Skriptum DE §6.4 with the edition-specific permission table; CLARIAH and Notes EN, "Prepare the Working Environment before the First Prompt". *Literature.* Saltzer and Schroeder 1975 (DOI 10.1109/PROC.1975.9939), proposed anchor for the principle, not cited in the scripts.
- **Intervention Point and Escalation Condition.** German Menschliche Interventionspunkte, Eskalationsbedingungen (Skriptum DE §6.1 and §6.6). The defined places at which an agentic run hands back to a person, named as contradictory requirements, missing disciplinary grounds, changes that are hard to reverse, sensitive resources, modelling decisions with disciplinary consequences, and validation and acceptance. *Source.* Skriptum DE §6.6 with the reversibility requirement for intermediate states; Notes EN, "Why Multi Step Work Must Be Organised". *Literature.* Anthropic 2024, "Building effective agents", on human checkpoints and guardrails.
- **Subagent.** German Subagent (Skriptum DE §6.5; Deck). A delegated agent instance with a bounded partial task, its own fresh context and a defined return format, used to keep large volumes of intermediate information out of the parent context and to run parallelisable checks. It is an architectural pattern without a formal standard, and more agents raise coordination and verification effort. Independent review distributes inspection and exposes disagreement, while model agreement produces no truth by voting. *Source.* Skriptum DE §6.5 with four edition-specific subagent roles; Notes EN, "Subagents" and "Subagents and Epistemic Infrastructure"; Deck. *Literature.* No standard and no citation in the mined texts; the Deck states that the subagent is the one item among the five agent concepts without a formal specification.
- **Model Routing.** German Model Routing, untranslated (Deck). The assignment of different phases of an agentic workflow to different models and inference budgets, typically a stronger reasoning model for planning and review and a faster model for implementation, treated as an engineering decision that changes as models change. *Source.* Notes EN, "Model Routing" with the sequence from research through specification, implementation and review to revision; Deck. *Literature.* Practitioner provenance, no canonical source.
- **Model Context Protocol (MCP).** German Model Context Protocol (MCP), untranslated (Skriptum DE §6.5; Deck). An open standard connecting AI applications with external tools, data sources and workflows through common client and server interactions. It addresses an integration problem and decides nothing about relevance, reliability or permissibility of a resource. *Source.* Skriptum DE §6.5 with footnote 5 on implementation-dependent permission architecture; Notes EN, "Model Context Protocol (MCP)"; Deck, with the M-times-N argument. *Literature.* Model Context Protocol specification (modelcontextprotocol.io).
- **Agent2Agent Protocol (A2A).** German Agent2Agent (A2A), untranslated (Deck); the Skriptum speaks of Agent-to-Agent-Protokollen (§6.5). An open protocol for discovery, task management and result exchange among independent agents without exposing internal memory, tools or proprietary logic, complementary to MCP in standardising agent-to-agent rather than agent-to-tool interaction. *Source.* Notes EN, "A2A (Agent to Agent)"; Skriptum DE §6.5 with the four methodological questions on responsibility, handover, conflict and decision authority; Deck. *Literature.* Agent2Agent Protocol specification (a2a-protocol.org).
- **Epistemic Infrastructure.** German Epistemische Infrastruktur (Deck). The arrangement in which knowledge documents, source data, schemas, tests, software tools, agents, project state and human judgment together establish the conditions under which a generated representation can be inspected, criticised, validated and accepted for a stated purpose. The concept carries the conclusion of both English texts. *Source.* CLARIAH, "AI-Supported Research Data Workflows & Epistemic Infrastructures" and "Agentic Engineering and AI Harnesses"; Notes EN, "Critical Expert and Epistemic Infrastructure"; Deck. *Literature.* Pollin, *Promptotyping* (monograph, review draft 0.9, 2026); Edwards et al. 2007, proposed wider anchor for the infrastructure-studies reading, which the scripts do not invoke.

### Promptotyping

- **Promptotyping.** German Promptotyping, untranslated (Skriptum DE, chapter 7). An iterative, document-based method for developing project-specific digital research artefacts with LLM-based agents, in which documented project understanding, artefact, checking procedures and bounded grounds of acceptance develop together. The cycle runs from project knowledge through working context and agentic implementation to a digital research artefact, its checking and the revision of the project knowledge. *Source.* Skriptum DE §7.1 with figure 11; CLARIAH, "Promptotyping"; Notes EN, "Promptotyping as an Iterative Knowledge Loop". *Literature.* Pollin, *Promptotyping* (monograph, review draft 0.9, 2026); Pollin 2025, "Promptotyping. Von der Idee zur Anwendung"; Pollin, Steyer, Scholger and Schiller-Stoff 2025, DHd (DOI 10.5281/zenodo.14943178); Pollin 2026, L.I.S.A.; DHCraft, Promptotyping specification. *Editorial decision 2026-08-20.* The monograph in review is added as an anchor. It is the load-bearing source for working context, project knowledge base, distillation, acceptance and epistemic infrastructure, and the earlier matrix did not list it. Until publication it is cited as a review draft.
- **Promptotyping Phase Model.** German Preparation, Exploration, Distillation, Implementation as chapter headings (Skriptum DE §7.2 to §7.6). The recurring work forms of the method, preparing sources and workspace into a reconstructible initial state, exploring what data and sources permit and constrain, distilling the developed understanding into maintained documents, and implementing the artefact so that gaps in the knowledge become visible. *Source.* Skriptum DE, chapter 7; CLARIAH, "Promptotyping" with the five-stage flow diagram; Notes EN, "Promptotyping as an Iterative Knowledge Loop". *Literature.* Pollin, *Promptotyping* (monograph, review draft 0.9, 2026); DHCraft, Promptotyping specification, which is the source of truth for the current phase model. The Deck's hands-on runs a different five-step sequence of Preparation, Planning, Feedback and Self Revision, Implementation, Verification; that sequence is a workshop exercise and does not restate the phase model.
- **Promptotype.** German Promptotype (Skriptum DE §7.10). The documented and referenceable state of one iteration, comprising the relevant state of the knowledge base, the artefact, the referenced data state, the documented checks, the open questions and the bounded grounds of acceptance. It is a sufficiently determined state to continue from, and acceptance attaches to it. *Source.* Skriptum DE §7.10. *Literature.* Pollin, *Promptotyping* (monograph, review draft 0.9, 2026).
- **Scholar-Centred Design.** German Scholar-Centred Design, untranslated (Skriptum DE §7.5; Deck). The orientation of development towards the research practices, interpretative tasks and responsibilities of the participating scholars, asking which scholarly distinctions an interface keeps visible beyond whether it is usable. *Source.* Skriptum DE §7.5; Notes EN, "Scholar Centred Design and Requirements Engineering"; Deck. *Literature.* Pollin 2025, *Modelling, Operationalising and Exploring Historical Information* (dissertation, Graz).
- **User Story.** German User Story, untranslated (Deck). A requirements format stating who acts, which capability is required and why it matters, used in the material as the bridge between structured research data and an implementable artefact, because it is readable by people and usable as agent context. *Source.* Notes EN, "As a … I Want to" with the liturgy and social-history examples; Deck, Scholar-Centred Design slide with worked stories. *Literature.* Pollin 2025, *Modelling, Operationalising and Exploring Historical Information* (dissertation, Graz).
- **Spec-Driven Development.** German spezifikationsgetriebene Entwicklung (proposal); the Deck uses the English term. The placement of an explicit specification between exploratory discussion and implementation, contextualised by the maintained knowledge base rather than written as a detached document, so that a reviewable object exists before code is produced. *Source.* Notes EN, "Spec Driven Development"; Deck, with the note that the specification is contextualised in the `knowledge` folder. *Literature.* Practitioner provenance, no canonical source.
- **Write-back.** German Write-back, untranslated (Skriptum DE §7.9). The defined operation of returning findings from implementation and checking into the responsible documents, so that new knowledge stays out of the chat, the code and one person's memory. Findings are examined before they are written back. *Source.* Skriptum DE §7.9 and §7.11; CLARIAH, "Promptotyping" as curated feedback; Notes EN, "Promptotyping as an Iterative Knowledge Loop" as curated write back. *Literature.* Pollin, *Promptotyping* (monograph, review draft 0.9, 2026).

## 5 Critical Perspectives and Governance

### Checking and acceptance

The material cuts the checking vocabulary two ways, and the cut of the Full Lecture Notes is canonical. *Editorial decision 2026-08-20.* Technical Verification means conformity to specified or formalised requirements and Validation means adequacy to source, purpose and disciplinary context. The CLARIAH lecture notes swap the two words, which is documented as an instance variant of that profile rather than as a second vocabulary. A text in the CLARIAH wording maps onto the canonical wording as follows.

| Canonical (Full Lecture Notes) | CLARIAH wording |
| --- | --- |
| Evaluation | Evaluation, narrower, measurement against an explicit criterion |
| Technical Verification | Validation |
| Validation (scholarly, domain) | Scholarly verification |
| Acceptance | no corresponding term |

- **Verification Levels.** German Prüfstufen (proposal). The umbrella name of the earlier matrix for the ordered set of checks a generated artefact passes, retained here as a pointer to the four separate entries below. The material separates three kinds of object that a single ladder cannot hold together, a procedure (deterministic check, model-assisted review), a role with authority (Critical Expert) and a decision (Acceptance). *Source.* Skriptum DE §7.7; Notes EN, "Evaluation, Verification, Validation and Acceptance"; CLARIAH, "Evaluation, Validation and Scholarly Verification". *Literature.* Wei 2025, asymmetry of verification, for the ordering principle; Zheng et al. 2023 (arXiv:2306.05685); Pollin 2025, "Promptotyping mit Claude Sonnet 4"; Anthropic 2024, "Building effective agents".
- **Evaluation.** German Evaluation (Skriptum DE §3.5 for prompt evaluation). Measurement or assessment of outputs, models or workflows against explicit criteria, quantitative or qualitative, including benchmark scores, error rates, task-completion measures and structured expert review. *Source.* Notes EN, "Evaluation, Verification, Validation and Acceptance"; CLARIAH, "Evaluation, Validation and Scholarly Verification". *Literature.* Zheng et al. 2023 (arXiv:2306.05685), proposed anchor for the model-based case; the Full Lecture Notes mark the broad usage in their own footnote.
- **Technical Verification.** German Technische Verifikation (Skriptum DE §7.7). The check whether an artefact conforms to specified or formalised requirements, through schema validation, deterministic mappings, test suites, numerical consistency checks and executable acceptance criteria, and therefore largely automatable because the conditions have been formalised. *Source.* Skriptum DE §7.7 with the edition-specific check list and the Verification Report template; Notes EN, "Evaluation, Verification, Validation and Acceptance". *Literature.* No external anchor; the Full Lecture Notes state that the distinction adapts established verification and validation terminology from software and systems engineering.
- **Validation (Scholarly or Domain Validation).** German Fachliche und wissenschaftliche Validierung (Skriptum DE §7.7). The check whether a representation, interpretation or artefact is adequate to the source, the purpose and the disciplinary context, which requires domain expertise and resists reduction to a deterministic test. A schema-valid document can encode a historically unjustified interpretation. *Source.* Skriptum DE §7.7 with its four guiding questions; Notes EN, same section; CLARIAH under the name scholarly verification. *Literature.* Pollin, *Promptotyping* (monograph, review draft 0.9, 2026). See the mapping note above for the CLARIAH wording.
- **Acceptance.** German Acceptance, untranslated (Skriptum DE §7.7, §7.10, §7.11). The responsible decision to use an identified state for a named purpose, stated purpose-bound, since a technically verified artefact can be unsuitable for research use and an interesting demonstrator can be unready for publication. Acceptance is separate from publication. *Source.* Skriptum DE §7.7 and the Acceptance Statement template with its sections for identified state, accepted-for purpose, technically verified, disciplinarily checked, not demonstrated, open questions and responsible decision; Notes EN, same section. *Literature.* Pollin, *Promptotyping* (monograph, review draft 0.9, 2026).
- **Critical Expert.** German Critical Expert, untranslated (Skriptum DE §7.8). The responsible disciplinary authority in a project, defined by function rather than by person, which decides which reading is defensible, whether a model is adequate to the source, whether an interface preserves scholarly distinctions and whether a state is accepted for a named purpose. Agents and validators supply evidence for that decision, and the role designs the epistemic conditions rather than manually checking every output. *Source.* Skriptum DE §7.8; Notes EN, "Critical Expert and Epistemic Infrastructure". *Literature.* Pollin 2025, "Promptotyping mit Claude Sonnet 4. Vibe-Coding erfordert den Critical-Expert-in-the-Loop".
- **Model-Assisted Review.** German Agentisches Review (Skriptum DE, figure 12 caption). The inspection of one model's output by a second model with access to the source and to executable tools, which can surface possible errors and direct human attention while reproducing the same mistake, introducing a new one or overstating confidence. Model agreement is no scholarly verification. *Source.* CLARIAH, "Model-Assisted Review inside an AI Harness"; Skriptum DE §7.7 with figure 12; Notes EN, "Subagents and Epistemic Infrastructure"; Deck, three-subagent TEI verification slide. *Literature.* Zheng et al. 2023 (arXiv:2306.05685).
- **Deterministic Check.** German Deterministische Prüfung (implied by Skriptum DE §7.7; the Deck uses deterministische Validation). A reproducible test over an explicit representation, such as recomputing row and column sums of a table or validating XML against a schema, which reveals internal inconsistency while establishing nothing about whether the source was interpreted correctly. *Source.* CLARIAH, "From Generated Text to Structured Data"; Notes EN, ".txt → JSON → CSV" with the pattern from probabilistic interpretation through structured representation to deterministic checking. *Literature.* Formulation of the teaching line.
- **Candidate Representation.** German Kandidatenrepräsentation (proposal), absent from both German sources. The status a model output holds before assessment, valuable as a plausible structured proposal while carrying no evidence of source fidelity. The material generalises the status beyond transcription to every representational transition in a workflow. *Source.* Notes EN, "Hands on 1 (Example Result)"; CLARIAH, "Example Result and Critical Review". *Literature.* Term of the teaching line.

### Openness and deployment

- **Openness as a Multidimensional Property.** German mehrdimensionale Offenheit (proposal), absent from the German sources, since the openness block exists only in the English lecture notes. The reading of openness as a set of separately gradable dimensions covering training data, documentation, training code, architecture, parameters, licences and access conditions, in place of a binary open or closed classification. *Source.* CLARIAH, "The Current LLM Capability Landscape". *Literature.* European Open Source AI Index (osai-index.eu), which evaluates fourteen dimensions.
- **Open-Weight Model.** German Open-Weight-Modell (proposal). A model whose trained parameters are available under a specified licence and can be downloaded, adapted and executed independently. The material keeps the term strictly separate from open source. *Source.* CLARIAH, "The Current LLM Capability Landscape" and the cyber-capability passage on diffusion risk; Notes EN, "Which Frontier Models and AI Technologies Do You Use?". *Literature.* Open Source Initiative, The Open Source AI Definition 1.0.
- **Open Source AI Definition.** German Open-Source-KI-Definition (proposal). The definition requiring the freedoms to use, study, modify and share an AI system together with access to the preferred form for modification, including sufficient information about training data, source code and model parameters. *Source.* CLARIAH, "The Current LLM Capability Landscape" with footnote 19. *Literature.* Open Source Initiative, The Open Source AI Definition 1.0.
- **Open-Washing.** German Open-Washing (proposal). The presentation of an AI system as open where significant restrictions on accessibility or reproducibility remain. *Source.* CLARIAH, "The Current LLM Capability Landscape" with footnote 21. *Literature.* Liesenfeld and Dingemanse 2024, FAccT (DOI 10.1145/3630106.3659005).
- **Local Deployment.** German lokaler Betrieb (proposal); the German Skriptum does not treat deployment. Running model inference on hardware controlled by the researcher or the institution, which can increase control over research data, inference configuration and availability while shifting responsibility for hardware, security, maintenance and operation to the institution. *Source.* CLARIAH, "The Current LLM Capability Landscape" with footnote 23; Notes EN, "Which Frontier Models and AI Technologies Do You Use?". *Literature.* No canonical source; the scripts name Ollama and LM Studio as environments.

### Risk and regulation

- **Governance Dilemma of Diffusion and Concentration.** German Governance-Dilemma zwischen Diffusion und Konzentration (proposal). The tension the scripts state for cyber capability, where highly capable open-weight systems can spread offensive capability beyond their developers while proprietary frontier systems concentrate the most capable systems and their infrastructure within a small number of companies and states. The source marks this as the author's own assessment, and the marking is preserved. *Source.* CLARIAH, "Frontier LLMs Asymmetrically Amplify Computational Research", cyber-capability passage. *Literature.* AI Security Institute, *Frontier AI Trends Report*.
- **Critical Cyber Capability.** German kritische Cyberfähigkeiten (proposal). The capability threshold at which a provider can no longer rule out that a model could develop functional zero-day exploits against hardened systems or devise and execute novel end-to-end attack strategies with minimal human direction. *Source.* CLARIAH, "Frontier LLMs Asymmetrically Amplify Computational Research" with footnote 31. *Literature.* OpenAI 2026, "Responding to the Next Frontier of Critical Cyber Capabilities", and the Preparedness Framework it refers to.

### EU AI Act vocabulary

The governance vocabulary of the teaching line lives in the VetMedAI workshop script, where module 5 is taught as a substantial block with its own exercise on distilling a knowledge document from the regulation. All eight entries are grounded in that script, in its short-form section "Der EU AI Act in Kurzform" and in the worked knowledge document it contains. Common literature anchor for all eight is Regulation (EU) 2024/1689 (OJ L, 2024/1689), https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/HTML/?uri=OJ:L_202401689.

- **Risk-Based Approach.** German risikobasierter Ansatz. The central organising principle of the Act, a four-tier classification of AI systems into unacceptable risk, high risk, limited risk with transparency obligations, and minimal risk, where the tier determines the intensity of the legal obligations. *Source.* VetMedAI, "Regulatory Architecture" of the worked knowledge document.
- **AI Literacy Obligation (Art. 4).** German KI-Kompetenz. The duty to ensure the skills, knowledge and understanding that let providers, deployers and affected persons make informed decisions about AI systems and become aware of opportunities, risks and possible harms. It is one of the two obligations that bind a research institution which uses rather than builds AI systems. *Source.* VetMedAI, "Der EU AI Act in Kurzform" and the definitions appendix.
- **Prohibited Practices (Art. 5).** German verbotene Praktiken. The eight absolutely prohibited categories of AI practice, among them manipulation through subliminal or deceptive techniques, exploitation of vulnerabilities, social scoring, predictive policing based solely on profiling, untargeted facial scraping, emotion recognition in workplace and education, biometric categorisation by sensitive attributes, and real-time remote biometric identification in public spaces for law enforcement. It is the second obligation immediately relevant to a using institution. *Source.* VetMedAI, "Der EU AI Act in Kurzform" and the prohibited-practices section of the worked knowledge document.
- **Provider (Art. 3(3)).** German Provider, Anbieter. A natural or legal person who develops or has developed an AI system or GPAI model and places it on the market or puts it into service under their own name or trademark, whether for payment or free of charge. The decisive criterion is responsibility for development and market placement. *Source.* VetMedAI, "Der EU AI Act in Kurzform" and the definitions appendix.
- **Deployer (Art. 3).** German Deployer, Betreiber. A natural or legal person using an AI system under their own authority in a professional capacity, with purely private non-professional use excluded. *Source.* VetMedAI, "Der EU AI Act in Kurzform" and the definitions appendix.
- **High-Risk System.** German Hochrisiko-System. The classification triggering the most demanding compliance regime, reached through two pathways, a safety component of a product covered by the harmonisation legislation of Annex I and requiring third-party conformity assessment, or a standalone system used in one of the eight sensitive domains of Annex III. A research system reaches it only when it leaves pure research for a regulated application area, for instance by qualifying as a medical device or in vitro diagnostic, or by being used in an Annex III context such as recruitment. *Source.* VetMedAI, "Der EU AI Act in Kurzform" and the high-risk section of the worked knowledge document.
- **Research Exemption (Art. 2(6)).** German Forschungsausnahme. The exemption for AI systems developed and used for the sole purpose of scientific research and development prior to market placement. The critical trigger is the transition from research to deployment rather than the subject matter of the research. *Source.* VetMedAI, scope section of the worked knowledge document and the virology example.
- **General-Purpose AI Model (GPAI).** German GPAI-Modell. The separate regulatory category of Chapter V, whose providers owe technical documentation, information to downstream providers, a copyright compliance policy and a sufficiently detailed summary of training data content, with additional obligations where systemic risk is presumed, which the Act ties to cumulative training computation above 10²⁵ floating-point operations or to Commission designation. *Source.* VetMedAI, GPAI sections of the worked knowledge document.
