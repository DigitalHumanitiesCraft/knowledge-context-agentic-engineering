# Critical Perspectives and Governance Source Map

Evidence-based intake for Module 05. This draft separates the accepted maintained-playlist sequence from literature and from current industry claims that need a critical counter-source. Candidate status alone does not place a source in the public playlist or teaching material.

## Teaching structure

### Digital control and cybernetics

This line introduces cybernetics as a history of feedback, prediction and control that supplies concepts for analysing digital governance. The focus is the relation between technical control systems, institutional authority and political subject formation.

Candidate sources include the short [Suhrkamp introduction to cybernetics](https://youtu.be/BZo-3ni4paU), the extended [Scobel discussion on digital control](https://youtu.be/cvZW3TD2jNE) and the book [*Kybernetik und Kritik*](https://www.suhrkamp.de/buch/anna-verena-nosthoff-kybernetik-und-kritik-t-9783518300794). The short video can establish the vocabulary. The discussion and book support a deeper seminar unit.

### Automation narratives and asymmetric amplification

This line examines claims that AI will automate programming or research. It separates a demonstrated technical result from a forecast about institutions, labour and scientific practice. The DHCraft essays on [Asymmetric Amplification](https://dhcraft.org/en/excellence/blog/Asymmetric-Amplification/) and [Asymmetric Amplifications and Epistemic Infrastructures](https://dhcraft.org/en/excellence/blog/Asymmetric-Amplifications-Epistemic-Infrastructures/) provide the project-specific analytical frame.

Current claim evidence includes the [research-automation interview](https://youtu.be/_JZT7oh9kCU), the [documented construction of a C compiler with parallel agents](https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/building-c-compiler), the [mathematical problem report](https://www-cs-faculty.stanford.edu/~knuth/papers/claude-cycles.pdf) and the [formal-proof demonstration](https://youtu.be/JHEO7cplfk8). These sources document capabilities and claims. They require independent evaluation before they support a general statement about research automation.

### Political economy, infrastructure and power

This line traces how model development depends on capital, compute, data, labour and political authority. It supports analysis of concentration, global competition, hidden labour, environmental cost and democratic accountability.

The compact public-video sequence can draw from the [Scobel analysis of the Californian ideology](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=64xK-SEl3nA), the [Stanford AI Index 2026 introduction](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ouIeC1fogvU), the [lecture on the political ecology of AI](https://youtu.be/h1WD-YpbhNQ) and the [TED talk on digital power](https://youtu.be/TZOoT8AbkNE). Longer optional routes include the [discussion of the AI investment race](https://youtu.be/QYeNESqKbSc), the [lecture on hidden labour](https://youtu.be/g4HtRyi_7-8) and the [philosophy discussion of the technology-billionaire worldview](https://youtu.be/PU0eQ4mjXK4).

The books *The AI Con* and *Empire of AI* support literature-based deep dives into hype, corporate organisation and institutional power. Bibliographic details must be verified before they enter published teaching material.

The first four entries establish ideological, political-economic and cybernetic frames. The final three narrow the route to competing AGI forecasts and the capital assumptions attached to them. The public library preserves this sequence and the source titles without editorial rewriting.

## Didactic use of opposing evidence

Industry and technical sources establish what a system demonstrated or what an institutional actor claims. Critical sources examine the inference from a capability demonstration to a prediction about knowledge work. Each teaching unit should preserve that source distinction in its slide labels and lecture-note citations.

Useful pairings include research-automation claims with the asymmetric-amplification framework, formal mathematical demonstrations with independent evaluation and verification frameworks, and investment-race narratives with political-economy analysis. This structure makes the evidential status of each claim visible.

## Editorial boundaries

- Treat current capability announcements and forecasts as dated evidence.
- Verify quotations against the primary recording or document before publication.
- Link to third-party visual material and do not copy its thumbnails or slide imagery into the repository without a documented reuse right.
- Keep research literature separate from the public playlist inventory.
- Record cross-module relations without duplicating a public video card.
