Making Estate Materials Digitally Accessible

Research Data Workflows with Large Language Models and Promptotyping

Abstract

Across four connected sessions, the workshop follows estate material from digitisation to a usable digital research artefact. The Wednesday session uses Stefan Zweig Digital to explain metadata, TEI XML, data modelling, authority data, repository ingest and publication. On Thursday, participants work with M³GIM objects, examine the role of large language models, extract and validate structured data, and develop a small browser-based application through Promptotyping.

Outline

  1. Wednesday, 17:00–19:00The Stefan Zweig Digital workflow from archival object to research platform
  2. Thursday, 11:30–13:00Large language models in research data workflows
  3. Thursday, 15:00–16:30OCR and schema-based information extraction with M³GIM objects
  4. Thursday, 17:00–18:30Promptotyping a digital research application

Learning objectives

  • Explain the transformations between a digitised source and a published research resource.
  • Identify suitable roles for large language models and define the required checks.
  • Create structured data while recording source relations, provenance and uncertainty.
  • Translate a reduced research question and checked data into application requirements.
