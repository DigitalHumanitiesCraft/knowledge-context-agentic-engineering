# Source-grounded content contract for Module 00 and Modules 01–05

## Purpose

This supplement fixes the learning question, conceptual scope and initial evidence routes for each hub. Public copy must be derived from the maintained teaching sources and translated by meaning. The definitions below are editorial boundaries for the first released version.

## Learning movement

`00 readiness → 01 system understanding → 02 bounded model call → 03 knowledge base and working context → 04 controlled work trajectory → 05 verification, authority and governance`

## Module 00 · Preparation and AI Coding Literacy

- **Definition:** the observable technical and methodological readiness required before work is delegated to an AI Harness.
- **Learning question:** What must I be able to observe, describe, execute and verify before I delegate a task?
- **Initial sequence:** describe task, material, result and decision; work with files, editor and terminal; run Python and localhost; inspect a harness through a reversible task; formulate requirements and acceptance criteria; read output and errors; apply domain judgement.
- **Route status:** the existing Preparation tutorial is available; the external German AI Coding Literacy curriculum is an explicitly labelled supplementary route.

## Module 01 · “Understanding” Large Language Models

- **Definition:** a functional account of what language models represent and generate, including uneven capability and the distinction between model, product, harness and infrastructure.
- **Learning question:** Which properties of an observed result follow from the model, its context and the surrounding system?
- **Initial sequence:** research-data representations; next-token prediction and transformers; tokens, embeddings and context; training, inference and assistant behaviour; jagged capability and failure; model, product and deployment boundary.
- **Current primary video count:** 5.
- **Workshop route:** all seven registered workshops.

## Module 02 · Prompt Engineering

- **Definition:** the specification of one bounded model call through task, material, requirements, constraints, output form and completion criteria.
- **Learning question:** How do I formulate a model call whose result can be inspected and compared?
- **Initial sequence:** distinguish prompt and context engineering; define task and material; state rules and output; add examples and schemas; compare variants; apply source and formal checks.
- **Current primary video count:** 1.
- **Workshop route:** all seven registered workshops.

## Module 03 · Knowledge and Context Engineering

- **Definition:** the maintenance of reusable knowledge and the deliberate selection of task-specific context.
- **Learning question:** Which knowledge should persist, which state belongs to the current task and which material should enter the working context now?
- **Initial sequence:** prompt, context and knowledge-base boundary; context windows, context rot and selection; knowledge acquisition; Knowledge Documents; selection and distillation; persistent instructions, Skills, retrieval and tools; integration of data and method context.
- **Concept map:** maintained knowledge flows through selection rules; task state flows through working context; both meet in task-specific context.
- **Current primary video count:** 6. Research Mission Control is a related route whose primary classification remains Module 04.
- **Workshop route:** all seven registered workshops.

## Module 04 · Agentic Engineering

- **Definition:** the design of observable multi-step work in which a model uses tools, maintains state and produces evidence for verification and acceptance.
- **Learning question:** How can an agentic work trajectory remain controlled, inspectable and recoverable?
- **Initial sequence:** model, harness, agent and state; action and tool feedback loop; permissions and reversibility; persistent rules and context; MCP, Skills and Plugins; Subagents, sessions and Worktrees; Promptotyping cycle; Research Mission Control and write-back.
- **Current primary video count:** 22.
- **Workshop route:** all seven registered workshops.

## Module 05 · Critical Perspectives and Governance

- **Definition:** the examination of evidence, authority, rights, infrastructure and power across AI-supported knowledge work.
- **Learning question:** Which claims, decisions and delegated actions require technical verification, domain validation, responsibility and governance?
- **Initial sequence:** candidate representation; evaluation, verification, domain validation and acceptance; critical expertise; provenance, rights, privacy and disclosure; openness, deployment and infrastructure; automation narratives, asymmetric amplification and cybernetics; canonical write-back.
- **Current primary video count:** 0. Preserve the empty state until accepted external sources are present in the maintained public playlist.
- **Workshop route:** six registered workshops; the HEDIT profile currently covers Modules 01–04.

## Editorial safeguards

1. Do not present a learning question as a slogan; every public paragraph must define a usable distinction or action.
2. Keep model capabilities separate from product and harness affordances.
3. Keep maintained knowledge separate from selected context while showing their operational relation.
4. Keep evaluation, scholarly verification, domain validation and Research Director acceptance as distinct evidence stages.
5. Preserve accurate route status: available, external, in preparation or absent.
6. Do not invent videos, workshop coverage, source maturity or public material links.
