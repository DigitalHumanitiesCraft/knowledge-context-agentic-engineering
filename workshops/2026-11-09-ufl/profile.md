## Profile

The register describes the audience as professionals from companies and institutions across disciplines. Six contact hours make this one of the shortest instances of the line, so the profile takes a narrow cut. ## Unit structure

Three units, proposed by the host for the course website and assessed as fitting by the operator. The wording below follows the host's own.

1. **Setup, terminal and agentic fundamentals.** Introduction to agentic engineering, the distinction between chatbot, assistant and agent, terminal and command line, installing and configuring the harness, permissions and security, and a first agentic task.
2. **Building a knowledge base and agentic engineering.** Analysing and preparing folder structures and files, building one's own knowledge base as the working basis for agents, developing concepts and process and project plans, defining human control points, and applying all of it to grant applications, document production and multi-step work. Operator feedback added the verification of agent results against one's own holdings as an explicit point of this unit.
3. **Light coding and own applications.** AI-supported coding, formulating requirements, having an implementation plan produced, small applications and prototypes, simple user interfaces, and judging the possibilities and limits of agentically developed solutions.

The hands-on work is progressive across the three units, running from the first agentic task in unit 1 to a small own application in the terminal in unit 3. The instance therefore installs and operates an agent, which sets its prerequisites and its room requirements.

A vocabulary split is settled and worth keeping stable. *Wissensnetz* is the public-facing term on the course website, while the workshop itself uses *Wissensbasis* and *Wissenssystem*.
