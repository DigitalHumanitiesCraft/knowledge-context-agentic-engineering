“Understanding” LLMs
According to Chalmers: 'My glass of understanding is half full.' I excel at explanations and verbal tasks, but I only simulate understanding and miss the phenomenal experience entirely... 	but can you really find out?!
PHENOMENAL understanding: The subjective “what-it-is-like” experience of understanding 	→ ✗ Absent in LLMs
EXPLANATORY understanding: Knowing and giving explanations 	→ ✓ LLMs often good at producing explanations 	    (but can confabulate/hallucinate)
USE understanding: Applying information to achieve goals
Verbal (answering questions) 	→ ✓✓ Strong
Behavioral (physical manipulation) → ≈ Limited (multimodal)
Reasoning (inference/planning)      → ≈ Partial (if they reason at all?)
Henk W. de Regt. Understanding Scientific Understanding. https://doi.org/10.1093/oso/9780190652913.001.0001Stochastic Parrots or Emergent Reasoners:  Can Large Language Models Understand?. June 2024. https://youtu.be/yyRzTL201zI

LLMs do next token prediction. They predict the next token in a sequence of tokens (context) based on their training data. Each predicted token becomes part of the context for the next prediction (autoregressive). This simple mechanism, scaled up massively, produces the behaviors we observe.

Anthropic researchers traced Claude 3.5 Haiku’s internal computations. They found genuine multi-step ‘reasoning’ (Dallas → Texas → Austin), planning ahead in poetry (activating rhyme words before writing lines), and language-agnostic representations across English, French, and Chinese. 

The model performs these computations automatically, without metacognitive awareness of its own processes.

Learning Objectives
How LLMs workExplain how LLMs process text (tokenization, embeddings, context windows, attention, etc.) and identify how these mechanisms affect output.
How to use LLMsInteract with LLMs using appropriate interfaces and understand the technical parameters that influence responses. Create and refine prompts using systematic techniques for (research) tasks. 
Critical discussion of LLMsDiscuss the technical limitations and broader implications of LLM use, including reliability issues (biases, hallucinations, etc.), data privacy, environmental impacts, and ethical, legal, educational, and social considerations.
How LLMs affect DH Research	Discuss how LLMs affect research methodologies in Digital Humanities and identify opportunities and risks for specific research contexts.
How to implement LLMs in DH ResearchDevelop and test AI-assisted workflows for potential LLM integration in research processes.

AI is a technological revolution that has been overshadowed by hype. Tech companies are making real, ongoing progress; however the extreme utopian and dystopian scenarios do not really help.
Implementation of GenAI remains complex and requires us to adapt collectively to something fundamentally new.
While all criticism of AI is justified, most critics underestimate its rapid evolution. Despite valid concerns, competitive pressures such as global rivalry, career advancement and research funding create irresistible incentives for adoption. What could stop this trend?
We must critically engage with technology evolving faster than our understanding. The question shifts from whether to use AI to how to preserve integrity and agency amid inevitable change.
I see no evidence to suggest that tech companies are incapable of building a AGI-like (or better AJI) systems.

The Weirdness of Mass Intelligence
Current State
1 billion+ regular AI users (ChatGPT: 700M weekly)
Cost per million tokens: $50 (2023) → $0.14 (2025)
Energy per query: ~0.0003 kWh (comparable to 2008 Google search)
Key Changes
Simplified interfaces replacing technical prompting
Partial free access to advanced reasoning models
Automated model selection (with implementation challenges)
Observed Impacts
Positive: Disease diagnosis, educational access, creative tools
Negative: Mental health risks, authenticity concerns, potential misuse
Institutional: Systems designed for "scarce intelligence" facing adaptation pressure
Open Question How should institutions built on assumptions of limited cognitive resources adapt to widespread AI availability?
